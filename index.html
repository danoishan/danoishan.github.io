03 / How I work

From outcome to operation.

A four-step pattern keeps the work grounded in the customer outcome while preserving clear decisions, handoffs and operating ownership.

01

Frame the outcome.

Agree on the customer outcome, constraints and evidence that would count as success.

02

Decide the path.

Clarify source, identity, dependencies and failure behavior. Recommend a path and record the trade-off.

03

Deliver it safely.

Define requirements, owners, acceptance criteria, specialist handoffs and release evidence.

04

Operate and scale.

Name monitoring and support owners. Use adoption and operating evidence to improve the next release.

A schedule is not a dependency map.

Dates tell you when. Dependencies tell you whether the date is even possible.

TECHNICAL NOTE

SDK vs REST is usually an ownership question.

Where the event originates and who owns the source of truth matter more than which transport looks easiest.

TROUBLESHOOTING NOTE

200 OK is not the outcome.

A successful request proves the transport accepted something. It does not prove identity, eligibility, journey behavior or customer outcome.

LAUNCH NOTE

Go/no-go should be boring.

Between the meeting room and the technical work.

At Vigorate, my official title is Senior Account Manager. Much of my work involves managing technical projects: understanding the client's problem, coordinating architects, engineers, data and QA teams, working through trade-offs and seeing projects through launch and ongoing support.
