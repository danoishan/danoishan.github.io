03 / How I work

From outcome to operation.

The method is simple to describe and harder to do well: understand the outcome, make decisions visible, test what matters, and leave the team with something they can actually run.

01

Frame the outcome.

Agree on the customer outcome, constraints and evidence that would count as success.

02

Decide the path.

Clarify source, identity, dependencies and failure behavior. Recommend a path and record the trade-off.

03

Deliver it safely.

Define requirements, owners, acceptance criteria, specialist handoffs and release evidence.

04

Operate and scale.

Name monitoring and support owners. Use adoption and operating evidence to improve the next release.

04 / Notes

Things I keep coming back to.

Short notes on the questions I keep coming back to when a project gets complicated.

OPERATING NOTE

A schedule is not a dependency map.

Dates tell you when. Dependencies tell you whether the date is even possible.

Read note →
TECHNICAL NOTE

SDK vs REST is usually an ownership question.

Where the event originates and who owns the source of truth matter more than which transport looks easiest.

Read note →
TROUBLESHOOTING NOTE

200 OK is not the outcome.

A successful request proves the transport accepted something. It does not prove identity, eligibility, journey behavior or customer outcome.

Read note →
LAUNCH NOTE

Go/no-go should be boring.

If the final launch meeting is where teams discover whether data, QA or rollback is ready, the control system failed earlier.

05 / About

Between the meeting room and the technical work.

At Vigorate, my title is Senior Account Manager. A lot of my day is technical delivery: figuring out what the client needs, bringing architects, engineers, data and QA teams into the right conversations, and working through the trade-offs before they become launch problems.

I manage scope, priorities, budgets, client communication and the handoffs between teams. Engineers and platform specialists own the production implementation; my job is to make sure the decisions and requirements survive the trip from meeting to launch. Work in loyalty, segmentation, consent and personalization has made me very aware of how quickly a small data gap can become a customer problem.

Away from the laptop: Black belt in Taekwondo, active in BJJ and Muay Thai, youth coach in both, solo skydiver, traveler, and reasonably competitive at Wordle. Open challenge to anyone ;)
