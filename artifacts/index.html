Which customer records could go?

A retailer had merged duplicate customer profiles in its CRM, while older records remained in Marketing Cloud across three brands. The team needed an agreed way to identify records that could be removed.

My contribution
I worked with the client and developers to define the eligibility criteria, sent each brand a file and count for approval, and coordinated the phased cleanup.
Decision
The client approved the initial population and a recurring cleanup. About 2.2 million records met the agreed criteria.
What went live
The final batch was completed and a monthly job ran with a safeguard for unusually large batches.

Planning an SAP cutover

Marketing Cloud journeys needed to pause for an SAP upgrade while critical messages remained live. I coordinated the affected journey versions with marketing and developers and gave the client a restart estimate tied to two IT confirmations.

Handoff
Marketing and developers reviewed the journey versions. Client IT owned confirmation that the SAP migration and preference centre were ready.
Evidence
Developers confirmed the listed journeys, including corrected versions, were paused within the agreed window.
Restart plan
A three-to-four-hour window would start after client IT confirmed both conditions.

Tracing a billing record

A bill existed in the source system, but its record failed to reach the customer journey. I worked with the billing provider and platform team to compare the source with the import.

Source
The bill was present in the inbound file.
Import
A comma in a customer name made the importer read an extra field and reject the row.
Next action
The client agreed to quote text fields. I asked the teams to reconcile the affected record before a resend.
