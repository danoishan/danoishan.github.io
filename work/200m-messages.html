Making 200 Million Messages Work

The programs I support handle more than 200 million email and SMS/MMS sends a year. I manage 20+ client workstreams per quarter, carrying work from the brief through implementation, testing, release and production follow-up.

The figures describe the scale of the client programs and workstreams I support. Push and in-app work are separate from the send count.

From a brief to a delivery plan

One request can involve the client, an architect, developers, data specialists and QA. I work with them to clarify what the customer should experience, which systems and teams are involved, and what has to happen before each team can start.

I bring the requirements and constraints to Solutions Consulting, who prepare the scope, estimates and budget with my input. I build the workback with the delivery teams, track dependencies and capacity, and bring changes and trade-offs back to the client.

For example, a journey may depend on a file from one team, eligibility rules from another and a decision from the client about who should receive the message. I connect those pieces in the plan, follow up on owners and dates, and explain what a delay means for the release.

Making the release testable

Audience rules, data freshness, consent, personalization and creative versions all affect what a customer receives. I turn those details into requirements and acceptance criteria, coordinate build and QA, and make sure the right people can review the evidence before approval.

When a file arrives late or a rule changes, I work with the specialists to understand what else is affected. I give the client the options and carry the agreed change through the schedule, testing and launch.

For one bilingual service notice, I reconciled the English and French audiences after duplicate removal, confirmed tests and coordinated a throttled release. Roughly 400,000 contacts remained. An hourly sending limit and an evening cutoff shaped a two-day plan. Read more about campaign delivery.

Staying with the work after launch

I follow up on releases in production, investigate issues with the teams closest to the systems, and keep the client informed through recovery. Reporting and team feedback also shape the next round of improvements.

That can mean tracing a record across a source file and Marketing Cloud, coordinating a recovery for people who missed a journey, or defining monitoring so a quiet billing journey is easier to spot. I stay involved until the team has an agreed next action and the client knows what happened.
