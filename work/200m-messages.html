Making 200 Million Messages Boring

Once a campaign is ready to send, I want to be able to name the audience file, the version QA tested and the person who can stop the next batch. Getting those answers is a big part of how I manage 20+ workstreams at once.

An operating pattern from my work at Vigorate. The audience decision below is a worked example. The volume and reported outcomes describe the wider programs.

Which audience is ready?

Suppose a new segment is ready to review and a send is already on the calendar. I’d use the approved file for this send if it still covers the intended audience and passes the release checks. I’d keep the new segment in parallel until we could explain any differences in who qualifies.

I use the same rule when the thing that changes is creative. In one bilingual automotive email release, an alt-text correction meant the revised asset needed a fresh test and approval. I coordinated the revised tests and timing with the client and distribution partners.

How I run the work across teams

Most problems start when two teams are working from different versions of the same plan. A late audience change can make an earlier test irrelevant. An urgent release can take the people already assigned to the next one. I make those conflicts visible early enough to change the plan.

What changes hands

From me
Scope, audience and data assumptions, acceptance criteria, open decisions and an escalation path.
From specialists
Implementation choices, build and test evidence, limitations and open defects.
From client and operations
Approval of the release, the expected customer impact, and a named monitoring owner.

A small copy revision may only need a named reviewer and a record of which version changed. A new audience source needs reconciliation, a consent check and test records for people who should stay out of the send. I also review capacity and budget so the extra work has somewhere to go.

What I ask before we go live

  • Can we reconcile the audience counts and explain the exclusions?
  • Does the approved version match the one QA tested?
  • Did we test someone who should never enter the journey?
  • Who can pause the release, and who checks the first results?

If a critical check fails, I put the recommendation and customer impact in front of the decision owner. A smaller release can work when the affected component can be isolated and the remaining work is ready.

Marketing needs to know who might get the wrong message. Engineering needs the exact source field and failing test. The sponsor needs the decision, the options and when they’ll hear from us again.

After launch, I make sure the operations team has the approved version, what to monitor and who to call if the first batch looks wrong.

Scale and reported results

Program scale
20+ concurrent enterprise workstreams per quarter. Programs supporting 200M+ annual email and SMS/MMS sends. Push and in-app were tracked separately.
Scope and timing
Reported 30% fewer late-stage scope changes and 20% shorter UAT-to-launch cycles across the programs.
Forecasting
Monthly forecast within 5% of actual delivery effort.
