Cleaning Up Customer Records Across Three Retail Brands

A retailer merged duplicate CRM profiles, but older records remained in Salesforce Marketing Cloud across three brands. I worked with the client and developers to agree which records could be removed, coordinate a phased cleanup and establish a recurring process.

Which record to keep The decision What went live

Which record could we remove?

I gave the client examples to trace in their CRM. They confirmed that the profiles had been merged and the surviving record still held the purchase history. Some of the old records still appeared opted in inside Marketing Cloud. That flag alone couldn’t tell us which record belonged on a send list.

With the client’s CRM team and our developers, I turned that finding into a rule we could check across all three brands. A candidate had to belong to the brand, be absent from its active email and SMS lists, and have no recent email activity or purchase. I sent the client a file and count for each brand to review before approving the deletions.

One cleanup or a recurring process?

About 2.2 million records met the agreed criteria. A one-time purge would deal with that backlog, but the CRM would keep merging profiles. I recommended a recurring cleanup. The plan brought each brand’s lists into the parent Marketing Cloud account, where our team could act on the approved records. The client approved both the initial population and the automated approach.

What went live

We processed the backlog in batches. When one batch was still running, I moved the next one and updated the client. In December 2025 I confirmed the final batch was complete. The total contact count was about 2 million lower than before the cleanup.

We put the ongoing job on a monthly schedule with a safeguard. If an unusually large number of contacts qualified, the job would stop before deleting them. That gave the operations team a chance to investigate an unexpected change in a brand’s list.
