The 80% Drop

After a CRM migration, reported form submissions fell by more than 80%. Website traffic didn’t fall the same way. I started tracing what happened between someone opening a form and their lead appearing in a report.

This composite draws on real delivery work. Some details and sequencing are combined to protect client confidentiality.

What changedTwo form setupsWhat I recommendedWhat would prove it

The visits were still there

The reporting suggested demand had disappeared. The traffic comparison gave us another possibility: customers were still arriving, and a step between the page and the report had stopped working reliably.

  1. PageDoes the form load in the real visit?
  2. CaptureDoes a submission create a Salesforce record?
  3. Customer dataCan we see the right consent and resolve the person?
  4. ReportingCan we tie the lead back to the right campaign?

Two ways to load a form

A standalone embed could work while a dynamically loaded form was slow or unpredictable. We compared the two on their actual pages and gave the website and Salesforce specialists a reproducible difference to investigate.

Standalone embed

A simpler page path gave the team a working reference for loading and submitting a form.

Dynamically loaded form

The browser script, iframe and Salesforce handler each needed to be checked where the form actually ran.

There were also gaps in consent visibility, identity and campaign attribution. Fixing the embed alone would leave those downstream questions open.

Prove capture first

For the unstable forms, the team moved toward a visitor-facing form connected to a Salesforce Form Handler. I recommended testing the real page and its downstream record before expanding to consent, customer data or attribution.

I kept the symptom, the test evidence, the recommendation and the acceptance criteria together. Web and Salesforce specialists owned the build and configuration.

The rollout I proposed started with a small set of forms and known test records, then added a monitoring and escalation owner before expanding to more pages.

What would count as recovery?

A test submission should create the expected Salesforce record with the right fields and consent. The team should be able to follow that same record to the intended campaign and report. I’d then compare real submission volume with the baseline over a defined period.

This case records the diagnosis and recommended validation sequence. The 80% figure is the reported decline that prompted the investigation. A measured post-fix recovery is outside the public record.
