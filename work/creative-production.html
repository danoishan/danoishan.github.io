One Offer, Many Versions

One of our bilingual automotive emails needed an alt-text correction. The updated file had to go through testing and approval again. I coordinated the revised tests and timing with the client and distribution partners.

From automotive account work. The email, video and social examples span the engagement.

The approval had to follow the file

English and French versions sometimes ran on different timelines. When a correction changed an asset, I had to identify the version it touched, make sure the right people tested it and get the revised timing to the client and partner.

  1. CorrectionUpdate the alt text in the affected HTML version.
  2. Fresh testCheck the revised asset before routing it for approval.
  3. Sign-offConfirm approval against the file the partner will send.

Reconstructed approval sequence. The example omits client assets and scheduling details.

A partner handoff included the approved HTML and language version, the render or seed-test feedback, and confirmation of who could deploy it. A preheader or personalization correction had to go through the same version-specific review.

What changed across formats

Vehicle campaigns also needed hero videos, shorter cuts, and static or motion social placements. Those teams worked from the same approved offer and messaging, with different review and export requirements.

Video and motion
Approved claims, cuts, formats and placement-specific exports.
Social
Placement, copy treatment, brand consistency and sign-off for the intended asset.
Bilingual email
Localized copy, subject and preheader, images, links, alt text and language-specific tests.

What made it out the door

I coordinated production and approval of video, motion and social creative and supported completed bilingual email releases through external partners. Creative specialists made the artwork and cuts. Developers built the HTML, and the distribution partners executed their sends.

The habit I kept from this work is simple: when I pass along feedback, I name the file it applies to. “Approved” is only useful if everyone knows which version we mean.
