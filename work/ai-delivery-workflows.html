From Brief to Testable Delivery

“Send a win-back message to lapsed customers” sounds like a complete brief until someone asks whether a customer who came back yesterday should still get it. I use Claude Code to surface questions like that before a ticket goes into build.

What I’d take back to the team

“Send the win-back message to lapsed customers.”
Question to settle
Which system defines lapsed, on what date, and which consent rule takes precedence?
Who checks
The client owns eligibility. Data verifies the source and how current it is.
“Don’t message people who came back.”
Test to add
Try someone who came back before entry, someone who came back during a delay, and a status update that arrives late.
Who checks
Engineering and QA confirm what the journey does in each case.
“Launch Friday.”
Decision to record
Which version is under test, when approval is due, and who can stop the release.
Who checks
The client and delivery lead review QA evidence and make the release decision.

How it fits into my work

I use Claude Code, subagents and reusable Agent Skills for separate passes on requirements, dependencies and test coverage. The useful output is usually a question I can bring to a specific person, or a test case we hadn’t written down yet.

I also share review instructions with colleagues for tickets, technical documentation and issue triage. The reusable part is the review method and the checks it asks for.

Who checks the answer

I check the draft against source documents and flag assumptions. A developer investigates code observations, QA validates the expected behaviour, and the client confirms business rules and sign-off. I use approved material for the review.
