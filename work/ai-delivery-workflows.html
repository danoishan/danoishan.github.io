Decision snapshot.

Operating choiceHuman-accountable delivery
AI delivery workflow summary
ProblemRequirements, platform notes and technical decisions are spread across documents and people.
ApproachUse focused AI-assisted reviews to surface questions, dependencies and tests before planning.
OwnershipI own the delivery framing and review path; engineers and platform specialists own technical authority and production implementation.
Result shown hereA reusable review process and illustrative output structure, not a claimed client efficiency metric.

AI can shorten the distance between a messy input and a useful first draft. It does not make an incomplete source correct or replace the people who own the system.

Break the problem into focused reviews.

I regularly use Claude and Claude Code, including custom subagents and Agent Skills, for research synthesis, requirements interpretation, implementation questions, draft QA scenarios and structured documentation. I also use AI-assisted code and implementation review to identify questions for technical specialists.

01 / REVIEW

What does the source say?

Extract requirements, assumptions, exclusions and unresolved decisions from available materials.

02 / DEPENDENCIES

What must happen first?

Surface integration, data, consent, QA and approval dependencies for sequencing.

03 / TESTABILITY

How would we know?

Draft acceptance criteria, negative cases, evidence needs and handoff questions.

Specialist work is not simply “accepted” because it appears in generated output. The point is to bring a better starting question to the right owner.

A generalized requirements-to-QA example.

Illustrative scenario: A CRM team wants to change who enters a lifecycle journey when status and consent data arrive from different systems. This example is synthetic and is intended to demonstrate a review format, not to claim that this exact engagement occurred.

The useful output is not a polished summary; it is a set of reviewable questions and testable conditions that specialists can accept, reject or refine.

Use explicit review gates.

Source claim can be traced to a document or owner
Assumptions are labelled, not hidden
Technical findings are validated with specialists
Sensitive client material stays within approved handling rules
Negative test cases are included
Final client decisions remain human-owned

AI-generated code observations may become investigation leads; they are not accepted as confirmed defects without reproduction, source evidence or developer validation.

Make useful patterns repeatable.

I also share reusable instructions and AI-assisted review patterns with colleagues for ticket preparation, issue triage, requirements questions and documentation. The principle is to preserve the review structure, not to turn one client's confidential material into a reusable prompt.

Useful reuse depends on small, maintainable instructions with an explicit input scope, expected output, validation step and human owner. A shared workflow is only valuable if another person can understand its limitations and use it safely.
