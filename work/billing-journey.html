The Bill That Couldn’t Go Missing

The billing record was there. The message never went out. A comma in a name field could shift the columns during import, leaving Salesforce Marketing Cloud with no usable row to put into the journey.

Based on cross-system delivery work. Client details are simplified. The recovery controls below were recommendations.

Where it stoppedWhat the file showedRecovery and prevention

Follow the missing record

We confirmed the record upstream. I brought the source, integration and Marketing Cloud teams together to trace it through the file and import and agree on what to check before resending.

  1. Source recordPresent in the billing system.
  2. File transferA resend needed a distinct batch or cycle identifier.
  3. ImportA delimiter inside a text field could shift the columns and reject the row.
  4. Billing journeyA rejected row never reached eligibility checks.

One extra comma

A failed row could go unnoticed upstream. The source team knew the bill existed, while the messaging team could only see records that made it past the import.

I kept the customer impact, batch identifiers and expected result in one investigation record so both teams could check their part against the same evidence.

Recover the record. Then fix the failure path.

I tracked the immediate recovery and the longer-term controls separately. A new batch identifier can get a file processed again. A stable business key and a duplicate check are what keep a customer from getting the message twice.

Immediate recovery
Identify the affected row, reconcile it to the source, and agree how a resend would be checked.
Prevention
Define how text is quoted, improve import logging, and route rejected rows to a named upstream owner.
Readiness test
Put a valid name with a comma through the import. Then reject a test record on purpose and verify someone can act on the exception.

I worked with the source and platform specialists on the diagnosis and set out what QA would need to prove for recovery and prevention.

The investigation identified where a record could drop out and what to do next. The alert and resend controls here describe proposed follow-up. Their production rollout is outside this case.
