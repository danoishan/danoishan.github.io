Case 04 · growth / operations / team leadership

125 Campaigns Across Six Provinces

Before I was leading CRM and MarTech programs, I learned the same core lesson in a different environment: scale only works when audience strategy, ownership and execution rhythm become repeatable.

EARLIER CAREER CASE · Based on my work at Jobs Canada Fair. Metrics reflect my resume history: 8-person team, six provinces, 125+ targeted email campaigns, +10% retention and +15% new-customer acquisition.

The program was national. The execution could not feel improvised.

Jobs Canada Fair operated across multiple provinces with a mix of employer outreach, attendee marketing, email campaigns, partner communication and in-person event delivery. The volume itself was manageable. The complexity came from repeating the work across markets without flattening everything into the same message or losing control of timing and ownership.

The real job was not “send more email.” It was create an operating rhythm that could repeat nationally while still feeling relevant locally.

Every market created another version of the same coordination problem.

Campaigns had different audiences, event dates, partners and operational dependencies. A missed handoff in one city could turn into a last-minute scramble; multiplied across a national calendar, that becomes the operating model.

I was leading an eight-person team, so the solution had to make priorities and ownership clear enough that the work did not depend on one person remembering every moving piece.

I treated each event as part of a portfolio, not a standalone project.

The repeatable structure was simple: know the audience, know the date that cannot move, know the dependencies and know who owns the next action.

STEP 1

Segment the audience

Match employer, candidate and partner communication to the market and event rather than defaulting to one generic national message.

STEP 2

Build the workback

Work backwards from fixed event dates so campaign, partner and operational dependencies surfaced early.

STEP 3

Run the rhythm

Use repeatable campaign and team routines so the next market could inherit what worked instead of starting from zero.

What I owned.

Marketing execution

  • Targeted email campaign planning and execution
  • Audience and market coordination
  • Digital campaign support
  • Partner communications
  • Follow-up sequencing

Program operations

  • Eight-person team leadership
  • National workback coordination
  • Event logistics and on-site activation
  • Priority and handoff management
  • Consistency across six provinces

Across the program, I owned strategy and execution for more than 125 targeted email campaigns. The work was not sophisticated because of a single platform feature. It was sophisticated because of the volume, repetition and need to keep audience relevance and operations aligned across markets.

The metrics mattered because they reflected repeatability.

The program contributed to a 10% increase in customer retention and a 15% increase in new-customer acquisition. Those are the headline numbers, but the more durable outcome was learning how to build a system that could support growth repeatedly across markets.

What carried forward into my technical delivery career.

The systems are different now, but the operating principles are almost identical. Today the dependencies may be identity, consent, source data, APIs or release environments instead of venue logistics and local campaign calendars.

The transferable skill is recognizing that scale changes the problem. Once work becomes concurrent, cross-functional and repeated, success depends less on individual heroics and more on a clear operating model.

This role was where I first learned to manage the portfolio, not just the task in front of me.
