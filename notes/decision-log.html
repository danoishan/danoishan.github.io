SEP 2026 · Working style

Write down the decision, not just the meeting.

Meeting notes preserve conversation. Decision logs preserve what the team is now building against.

Teams rarely disagree about what was said. They disagree about what it meant.

A week after a meeting, one person remembers a recommendation, another remembers an approval and a third remembers an open question. Traditional notes can document all three interpretations without resolving which one governs the work.

The durable artifact is the choice: what we decided, why, who owns it and what would cause us to revisit it.

What I want in a useful decision record.

Decision logMinimum useful fields
FieldWhy it matters
DecisionStates the choice in plain language
AlternativesShows what was consciously rejected
RationalePreserves the constraint or goal behind the choice
OwnerMakes accountability visible
AssumptionsExposes what must remain true
Revisit triggerPrevents endless relitigation while allowing new evidence

Decision logs are especially useful across system boundaries.

If marketing, data, architecture and development all leave a meeting with different assumptions about identity, source of truth or fallback behaviour, the disagreement may not appear until build or QA. A short written decision is cheaper than late rework.

The practical rule.

If a conversation changes architecture, scope, ownership, timing or customer behaviour, capture the decision separately from the meeting notes. The team should be able to find the current answer without replaying the meeting.
